    Chapter 00 · Permian–Triassic boundary

    The Great Dying

    252 million years ago, colossal volcanic eruptions in what is now Siberia poured lava and greenhouse gases into the world over thousands of years. Oceans warmed, turned acidic and lost their oxygen. It was the worst mass extinction in Earth’s history.

    Life nearly vanished. From a handful of survivors, a new world would be built.

    Chapter 01 · Early Triassic

    From the ashes, the archosaurs

    Among the survivors were the archosaurs, the “ruling reptiles”. Within a few million years they split into two great lines: one leading to crocodiles, the other to pterosaurs and dinosaurs.

    Their breakthrough was posture. Legs moved from sprawling out to the sides toward standing beneath the body, which made running more efficient and let the chest breathe while moving.

    Chapter 02 · Lineage decoder

    Reading the family tree

    Dinosaurs appeared around 233 million years ago. Almost immediately they split into two branches, told apart by the shape of the hip. The highlighted route traces the one line that never ended.

    *Placement of the earliest dinosaurs is still debated. Tree simplified; branch lengths are not to scale.

    01 · Hip architecture

    Saurischians kept a forward-pointing pubic bone. Ornithischians evolved one that points backward, like a bird’s.

    02 · Upright stance

    Legs held straight beneath the body gave dinosaurs an efficient stride and supported enormous body sizes later on.

    03 · The bird paradox

    Birds evolved from “lizard-hipped” theropods, not “bird-hipped” ornithischians. Their bird-like hips evolved separately.

    Period I · 252 → 201.3 MYA · 51 million years

    TriassicTriassic

    All land formed one supercontinent, Pangaea. Its vast interior was hot and dry, with seasonal monsoons along the coasts. The first dinosaurs were small, fast, two-legged animals, and for millions of years they were minor players.

    Field note Between about 234 and 232 MYA, the Carnian Pluvial Episode brought a million years or more of intense rainfall. Dinosaurs diversified rapidly in its wake.

    The end-Triassic extinction

    As Pangaea began to tear apart, the eruptions of the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province flooded the atmosphere with carbon dioxide. About three quarters of species vanished, including most of the crocodile-line rivals that had dominated the land. The dinosaurs survived, and inherited it.

    Period II · 201.3 → 145 MYA · 56 million years

    JurassicJurassic

    Pangaea split into Laurasia in the north and Gondwana in the south. The climate turned warm and humid, and forests of conifers, cycads and ferns spread across the land. Dinosaurs became the dominant land animals and grew to record-breaking sizes.

    The age of dinosaurs ended

    About three quarters of all plant and animal species on Earth disappeared, including every dinosaur that could not fly.

    Chapter 06 · The K–Pg boundary

    Written in stone

    All over the world, a thin layer of clay marks the moment. It is rich in iridium, a metal rare in Earth’s crust but common in asteroids. In 1980, Luis and Walter Alvarez used it to propose the impact theory. The buried Chicxulub crater, identified about a decade later, was the smoking gun.

    At the Tanis fossil site in North Dakota, fish were found with tiny glassy impact spherules lodged in their gills, likely killed within hours of the strike.

    Epilogue · Present day

    Not every dinosaur died

    One branch of small, feathered theropods survived the catastrophe. Their descendants are all around you: about 11,000 living species of birds. Every pigeon, penguin and hummingbird is a living dinosaur.

    With the giants gone, mammals spread into an emptied world. Sixty-six million years later, one of them is reading this.
